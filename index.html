Home/Journal

How a painting becomes a rug

An artwork is not a rug yet. These are the steps that carry a painting from the canvas to the loom.

Atelier

From canvas to design

The artwork is photographed or scanned at high resolution, then redrawn as a rug design. For a hand-knotted rug the design becomes a graph, where each square stands for a single knot, so every brushstroke has to be read at the scale of the weave.

Reducing the palette

A painting can hold thousands of tones. A rug works with a defined set of yarn colours, so the designer chooses the colours that carry the work and matches each one to yarn samples. Colours are then dyed to that palette.

Choosing the weave

Fine, detailed work suits hand-knotting, where density gives sharp edges and subtle blends. Bold, gestural work often suits hand-tufting, where pile can be carved to echo the movement of the brush. Mixing pile heights or adding a sheen yarn can bring out texture.

The strike-off

Before a full rug is made, a sample section called a strike-off is woven. It shows how the colours, scale and texture read in wool, and it is where the artist and the maker refine the details.

Scaling up

Once the strike-off is approved, the design is set out at its final size and woven. The finished rug is washed and finished, ready to stand alongside the original artwork.

Designing your first rug collection: a guide

A first collection is easier to make, and to sell, when it is planned as a set rather than a series of single rugs.

Atelier

Start with a story

Decide what ties the collection together: a place, a body of artwork, a material or a mood. A clear idea makes every later decision, from colour to naming, simpler.

Keep the range focused

Many first collections work best with a small number of designs that share a palette or a motif. A tight edit is easier to sample, photograph and present.

Build one palette

Choose a shared set of yarn colours across the collection. It keeps the designs related and makes sampling more efficient.

Plan your sizes

Think about where the rugs will live and offer the sizes those rooms need. Custom sizes can sit alongside a few standard ones.

Match the weave to the price point

Hand-knotted rugs take longest to make and suit heirloom pieces; hand-tufted and handwoven rugs suit larger sizes and broader price points. The weave shapes both the look and the cost.

Sample before you launch

Strike-offs let you check colour, scale and texture before committing to production. Budget time for at least one round of changes.

Hand-knotted, hand-tufted or handwoven?

Each technique produces a different surface, a different lifespan and a different price. Here is how they compare.

Craft

Hand-knotted

Each knot is tied by hand around the warp threads of a loom, row after row, then the pile is trimmed. It is the slowest method and the most durable, with the finest detail. Seraj Rugs works in the Tibetan knotting tradition, known for thick pile and carved texture.

Hand-tufted

Yarn is punched through a backing cloth with a hand-held tufting tool, then the pile is sheared and often carved. Tufting is faster than knotting, suits bold designs and large sizes, and gives a dense, plush surface.

Handwoven

Weft yarn is woven through the warp on a handloom. Handwoven rugs are often flatter and lighter, with tonal, textured surfaces, and many are reversible.

Flatweave

Kilims and dhurries have no pile at all. They are light, easy to move and suited to stripes, borders and graphic patterns, and they layer well over other rugs.

Which to choose

For an heirloom piece or fine detail, choose hand-knotted. For bold designs, large sizes or many rugs on a schedule, consider hand-tufted. For texture and a relaxed feel, look at handwoven and flatweave.

Nepal or India: where to make your rugs

Seraj Rugs works across Nepal and India. Where your rugs should be made depends on the weave, the quantity and the timeline.

Trade

Nepal: the Tibetan knot

Nepal is known for Tibetan hand-knotting: dense, hand-tied pile with carved texture and soft, deep colour. It is the natural choice for fine hand-knotted rugs and statement pieces.

India: volume and variety

India offers a wide range of techniques, including hand-tufted, handwoven and flatweave as well as hand-knotted, and the capacity for larger volumes. Seraj Rugs works in Bhadohi, one of India’s best-known carpet weaving centres.

What to weigh up

Consider the technique your design needs, the number of rugs, your installation date and the landed cost, including freight and duties to your country.

Compare both

Nepal for fine hand-knotting and carved texture; India for large volumes and variety. We quote both side by side, with landed cost, so you can choose with the full picture.

Our story

Three generations, two countries, and the hands of hundreds of weavers.

A family, a craft, and a promise that began in Nepal and grew across the border.

  1. 01

    Roots in Nepal

    Our story begins in Nepal, where the Tibetan knot is tied by hand. From the start the work was the same: wool, a loom, and hands that knew what to do.

  2. 02

    Across the border

    Our founder, Shahid Ali Shah, carried that craft to India and pioneered Tibetan hand-knotted rugs there. He worked with about 4,000 artisans and helped turn a trade into an art.

  3. 03

    Seraj Rugs sold through major buying houses in Europe, especially in Germany, where buyers check quality closely and remember who delivers.

  4. 04

  5. 05

    Three generations now work in the business. We make hand-knotted, hand-tufted, handwoven and flatweave rugs in our own workshops in Nepal and India, for clients in the USA, Canada, Europe and the Gulf.

  6. 06

    Tomorrow

    We keep growing: more looms, more weavers, more skills. Every new loom is a place for the next weaver, and every rug is made by hands we trained.
